# Source acquisition — commission brief

**Two retrieval tasks, one researcher, probably one library visit.**
Prepared 17 August 2026. Both are off the critical path and independent of the
cultural review, so they can start immediately and in parallel.

## Who we need

A researcher who reads classical Chinese and has library or licensed digital
access to Ming and Qing physiognomy texts. Three of the highest-value primary

Estimated: **two to four days**, assuming access exists.

## Task 1 — Three Sections: locate the primary source

**Priority: high. This is our weakest evidential position.**

### The problem

The entire basis for the Three Sections construct — the idea that balanced
facial thirds are auspicious — is one eight-character maxim, known to us only
through two secondary sources, from a text whose authorship is legendary.

The maxim: 三停平等，富貴榮顯 — "when the three sections stand equal, wealth,
rank and honour are manifest". Attributed to Ma Yi Shen Xiang (麻衣神相 /
麻衣相法), a text ascribed to "the Hemp-Robed Daoist", said to be Chen Tuan's
teacher. Neither the figure's historicity nor the text's date is established by
anything we retrieved.

We could not retrieve any Ming or Qing edition body text. We also found
conflicting, unresolved evidence about whether Shen Xiang Quan Bian (神相全編)
contains a dedicated Three Sections chapter at all — note that its table of
contents does contain 十三部位總歌, which is a *different* facial-division scheme
and must not be conflated with this one.

### Deliverable

1. A photograph or licensed scan of the page carrying the maxim in a named
   recension — **or a documented negative result**, that a named recension does
   not contain it.
2. Edition identified: printer or 藏板, date, juan and folio.
3. The surrounding passage transcribed, so we can see whether the maxim is a
   heading, a verse line, or a commentator's gloss. **This distinction matters
   to us:** a commentator's gloss is a different kind of claim from a text's own
   statement, and we would present it differently.
4. A note on whether the recension carries a Three Sections section at all, or
   only the maxim in passing.

### Where to start

- **Gujin Tushu Jicheng, Yishu dian, physiognomy division** on Chinese
  Wikisource is public-domain-tagged and *was* retrievable for our other
  material. First place to look, because it worked when the others did not.
- A university East Asian collection holding a Ma Yi Xiang Fa recension. This
  is the route that produces a citable folio.
- The Chinese Text Project holds the text but blocks automated retrieval and
  prohibits bulk download. **Manual consultation under its terms is fine;
  scraping is not.**

### Why the negative result is genuinely useful

If the maxim is not in any recension checked, the construct's stated basis is
wrong, and we would rather find that ourselves than have a reviewer find it.
A division of the face into three, presented without the fortune claim, remains
a legitimate heritage entry — it is the *claim* that needs the source, not the
division. Do not pad a negative result.

## Task 2 — Twelve Palaces: retrieve the chapter body

**Priority: high, and it unblocks a question already with the cultural reviewer.**

### The problem

We verified that Shen Xiang Quan Bian contains a chapter titled 十二宮相論 at
position 9 in its table of contents, in a Ming 致和堂藏板 edition of twelve juan
plus one opening juan. **We could not retrieve the chapter body.** Three hosts
blocked us: the Chinese Text Project returned HTTP 403, and two others are
robots-blocked.

So we can prove the chapter exists. We cannot quote it, and we have no
per-palace locators.

### Deliverable

1. Transcription of the 十二宮相論 chapter, with juan and folio references.
2. The twelve palace names **as they appear in the source**, not as they appear
   in modern circulation. This is the specific thing we need: two of the names
   in circulation are 妻妾宮 and 奴僕宮, and modern sources substitute 夫妻宮 and
   others. We need to know what the text actually says before we decide how to
   handle it.
3. Any indication in the text of how the palaces map to facial regions.

### Context the researcher should have

Our cultural reviewer has been asked whether those two palaces should be
suppressed, shown literally, or modernised. **They are currently being asked
about names we hold only from secondary sources.** Getting them the chapter body
materially improves the quality of the answer we get back. If this task
completes before the review does, send it straight on.

### A hazard worth flagging

All twelve palace names are shared verbatim with the twelve houses of Zi Wei Dou
Shu natal astrology — a completely different system. Search results for the bare
term are dominated by the astrology. Please confirm that anything retrieved is
from a physiognomy context.

## Common conditions

- **Public-domain sources only.** Modern collated editions (點校本) attract
  copyright in China following the 2013 Beijing First Intermediate People's
  Court decision, so they are reference material for us, not source material.
  If the only accessible copy is a modern collated edition, tell us — do not
  transcribe from it as though it were the source.
- **Record what you could not get.** A documented gap is worth more to us than a
  filled one we cannot trace.
- Deliverables are hashed into our evidence manifest, so please supply files
  rather than summaries.

## What these do not close

Neither task clears a content family. They satisfy part of one of six
requirements — a named edition and locator per tradition claim. Cultural review,
contributor determination, legal approval and hashing remain open regardless of
the outcome.
